# BC IEP policy context

A short, sourced summary of the public British Columbia policy that Altamyra works within. Every factual line links to the official page or PDF the team read. Quotes are exact. Anything we could not confirm is marked **unverified** or **team belief**.

This is a product reference, not legal advice. Districts add their own local rules on top of provincial policy.

### A note on the manual's name

The older title, *Special Education Services: A Manual of Policies, Procedures and Guidelines*, has been replaced. The [resources page](https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/education-training/k-12/teach/resources-for-teachers/inclusive-education) says the ministry "updated the language within the Special Education Services: A Manual of Policies, Procedures and Guidelines and renamed the document as Inclusive Education Services: A Manual of Policies, Procedures and Guidelines", and that these changes "do not alter the policy intent". The 2024 law and manual also use the phrase "students with disabilities or diverse abilities" where older documents said "special needs". Altamyra copy should use the current terms.

## What an IEP is

The [manual's glossary](https://www2.gov.bc.ca/assets/gov/education/administration/kindergarten-to-grade-12/inclusive/inclusive_ed_policy_manual.pdf) defines it as:

> "An Individual Education Plan (IEP) is a documented plan developed for a student with disabilities or diverse abilities that describes individualized goals, adaptations, modifications, the services to be provided, and includes measures for tracking achievement."

The [IEP Order](https://www2.gov.bc.ca/assets/gov/education/administration/legislation-policy/legislation/schoollaw/e/m638_95.pdf) (section 1) says an IEP "includes one or more of the following":

- learning outcomes that are "different from or in addition to the expected learning outcomes" for the course, subject and grade;
- "a list of support services required for the student to achieve the learning outcomes established for the student";
- "a list of the adapted materials, or instructional or assessment methods required by the student".

The [manual](https://www2.gov.bc.ca/assets/gov/education/administration/kindergarten-to-grade-12/inclusive/inclusive_ed_policy_manual.pdf) (section C.3) adds that an IEP "should also include" the student's present levels of performance, the setting, the names of the staff providing the program and services, "the period of time and process for review of the IEP", "evidence of evaluation or review, which could include revisions made to the plan and the tracking of achievement in relation to goals", and transition plans. Where goals differ from grade expectations, they should "be accompanied by measurable objectives developed for each goal to enable IEP review and evaluation."

The manual also says the IEP "serves as the basis for reporting the student's progress" and is "a tool for tracking individual student learning in terms of agreed- upon goals and objectives."

## Who needs an IEP

From the [IEP Order](https://www2.gov.bc.ca/assets/gov/education/administration/legislation-policy/legislation/schoollaw/e/m638_95.pdf), section 2(1):

> "A board must ensure that an IEP is designed for a student with disabilities or diverse abilities, as soon as practical after the student is so identified by the board."

Section 2(2) gives the exception. It applies where the student needs "no adaptation or only minor adaptations", the expected learning outcomes "have not been modified", **and** the student needs "25 hours or less remedial instruction, by a person other than the classroom teacher" in a school year. The manual's "Who must have an IEP?" section repeats these three conditions joined by "and".

Note: the manual's earlier "Planning" section (section A) lists the same three exceptions joined by "or". The IEP Order is the legal text, so we read the conditions as all three together. Team reading, not a Ministry ruling.

Who does the work: the [manual](https://www2.gov.bc.ca/assets/gov/education/administration/kindergarten-to-grade-12/inclusive/inclusive_ed_policy_manual.pdf) says "the principal of the school should ensure that for each such student a case manager is appointed to co-ordinate development, documentation and implementation of the student's IEP."

## Review at least once a school year

From the [IEP Order](https://www2.gov.bc.ca/assets/gov/education/administration/legislation-policy/legislation/schoollaw/e/m638_95.pdf), section 4(a), the board:

> "must ensure that the IEP is reviewed at least once each school year following the year the IEP is developed and, where necessary, it is revised, or cancelled"

Section 5 adds that the board "must offer each student learning activities in accordance with the IEP designed for that student."

The [manual](https://www2.gov.bc.ca/assets/gov/education/administration/kindergarten-to-grade-12/inclusive/inclusive_ed_policy_manual.pdf) says IEP documentation provides evidence that the parent and/or student were offered consultation, that "the student is receiving learning activities in accordance with IEP", and that "the IEP is reviewed at least once each school year."

## Parent and student consultation

From the [IEP Order](https://www2.gov.bc.ca/assets/gov/education/administration/legislation-policy/legislation/schoollaw/e/m638_95.pdf), section 4(b), the board:

> "must offer a parent of the student, and where appropriate, the student the opportunity to be consulted about the preparation of an IEP."

The [manual](https://www2.gov.bc.ca/assets/gov/education/administration/kindergarten-to-grade-12/inclusive/inclusive_ed_policy_manual.pdf) adds: "Parents must be given the opportunity to be consulted in the planning process, and should receive a copy of the IEP. To the extent possible, the student should also participate in the process." It also says "The IEP should document instances where services are offered but the parent or the student refuses them."

For families with a different home language, the manual (section C.7) says teachers should, "when language is a barrier, use an interpreter for communicating with the parent or the student to assist with meetings, assessments and planning sessions."

## Adaptations vs modifications

This is the line the Adapt/Modify Guardrail enforces. From the [manual's glossary](https://www2.gov.bc.ca/assets/gov/education/administration/kindergarten-to-grade-12/inclusive/inclusive_ed_policy_manual.pdf):

> "Adaptations are teaching and assessment strategies especially designed to accommodate a student's needs so they can achieve the learning outcomes of the subject or course and to demonstrate mastery of concepts. Essentially, adaptations are "best practice" in teaching."

> "Modifications are instructional and assessment-related decisions made to accommodate a student's educational needs that consist of individualized learning goals and outcomes which are different than learning outcomes of a course or subject."

The glossary also says modifications "should be considered for those students whose disabilities or diverse abilities are such that they are unable to access the curriculum" and "Using the strategy of modifications for students not identified as disabilities or diverse abilities should be a rare practice." It adds that "Adaptations do not represent unfair advantages to students."

The policy section (section A) gives shorter versions with examples: adaptations "may include alternate formats (e.g., braille, books-on- tape), instructional strategies (e.g., use of interpreters, visual cues and aids) and assessment procedures (e.g., oral exams, additional time, assistive technologies)"; modifications are "learning outcomes that are substantially different from the regular curriculum".

In short: an adaptation changes **how** a student learns or shows learning; a modification changes **what** the student is expected to learn.

The manual (section C.5) also says "Use of adapted or modified assessment supports should be noted in the student's IEP."

## Competency-based IEPs (CB-IEP)

What we could verify:

- Templates are a district choice. The [resources page](https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/education-training/k-12/teach/resources-for-teachers/inclusive-education) says districts and independent school authorities "have the autonomy to develop their own IEP template, or to use one of the templates available in the Student Services module in MyEducationBC. Specific templates are not mandated."
- Competency-based IEPs are in use and audited. A [2021/22 Ministry special education enrolment audit of one school](https://www2.gov.bc.ca/assets/gov/education/administration/resource-management/compliance-audits/2122/2122-se-tla-audit-report.pdf) found "The IEPs in the Competency-Based format did not contain a method of measuring progress towards IEP objectives." A [2023/24 audit of one district](https://www2.gov.bc.ca/assets/gov/education/administration/resource-management/compliance-audits/2324/audit-reports/k12/2324_sd44_k-12_audit_report.pdf) noted "the first year the District implemented Competency-Based IEPs" and recommended staff take "a Ministry sanctioned workshop on writing effective competency-based IEPs."

**Unverified:** the exact sections of the CB-IEP template in MyEducation BC (for example a strengths-based student profile, supports, and goals tied to the Core Competencies). We found only district pages describing it and did not read them in full. The MyEducationBC.info training site could not be reached (TLS certificate error). Treat any CB-IEP field list in the code as a team belief until someone checks the template.

Takeaway the sources do support: whatever the template, goals need measurable objectives and a method of measuring progress.

## Reporting for students with IEPs

The [K-12 Student Reporting Policy](https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/education-training/k-12/administration/legislation-policy/public-schools/student-reporting) applies to everyone:

> "All students, including those who may also have individual learning goals identified in a document such as an Individual Education Plan (IEP) or an Annual Instruction Plan (AIP), will receive communications of their learning in the same format, and on the same schedule as their peers."

The same page sets the schedule: "at least 5 communications of student learning", of which "At least three of these communications will be written in the form of a Written Learning Update or Summary of Learning" and "At least two of these communications will be in the form of an Informal Learning Update."

**Most students with IEPs are reported on the curriculum, not their IEP goals.** The [Inclusive Education Reporting Guide](https://curriculum.gov.bc.ca/sites/curriculum.gov.bc.ca/files/pdf/reporting/k12-reporting-inclusive-education-reporting-guide.pdf) says: "Unless local reporting practice requires it, there is no requirement to report on IEP goals for students who are being assessed in relation to the Learning Standards of the curriculum." It also says a student with an IEP "should not be assessed automatically as "Emerging," "Developing" or at a lower letter grade and percentage because they use supports".

**Goal-based reporting is required for the rare students assessed only on IEP goals.** From the [reporting policy](https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/education-training/k-12/administration/legislation-policy/public-schools/student-reporting), for students "assessed and evaluated only on individualized learning goals as outlined in their Individual Education Plan (IEP)":

- Written Learning Updates and the Summary of Learning "do not need to include a scale indicator or letter grade and percentage."
- "Written feedback is required that clearly explains the student's progress made towards their individualized learning goals and areas for further growth."
- If a scale or grade is used, "it must be noted that the student is being evaluated in relation to their individualized learning goals as outlined in their IEP and not the Learning Standards of the curriculum".

The [guidelines](https://www2.gov.bc.ca/assets/gov/education/administration/kindergarten-to-grade-12/k-12-student-reporting-policy-communicating-student-learning-guidelines.pdf) add that "Students with disabilities and diverse abilities whose learning is supported by individualized learning goals must have an IEP that outlines their goals and the methods for evaluating the goals", and that individualized goals "are set by teachers in consultation with students, parents, caregivers, and other student support team members". The [reporting guide](https://curriculum.gov.bc.ca/sites/curriculum.gov.bc.ca/files/pdf/reporting/k12-reporting-inclusive-education-reporting-guide.pdf) says descriptive feedback "should be concise (bullets are okay) and in parent/caregiver-friendly language" and "should not summarize lesson plans or the Learning Standards of the curriculum".

It also notes that teachers providing support services "may wish to send information about the student's growth and needs at the same time as the Learning Updates. However, this is not a formal reporting requirement."

## MyEducation BC

The [Ministry's MyEducation BC page](https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/education-training/k-12/administration/program-management/myeducation-bc) says MyEd BC "is British Columbia's secure student information system (SIS), supporting K-12 education across all public school districts, most independent schools, some Independent First Nations schools, and the Yukon", and that it handles "everything from attendance and scheduling to Learning Updates and Individual Education Plans (IEPs)."

The Ministry's [privacy impact assessment template for MyEducation BC](https://www2.gov.bc.ca/assets/gov/education/kindergarten-to-grade-12/support/myeducationbc/pia-myeducation-appendix-a.pdf) says "School staff, counsellors and/or special education practitioners enter special education information and Individual Education Plans for students." The [reporting guidelines](https://www2.gov.bc.ca/assets/gov/education/administration/kindergarten-to-grade-12/k-12-student-reporting-policy-communicating-student-learning-guidelines.pdf) list "Provide reporting forms on MyEducation BC" as a Ministry responsibility.

**Team belief, not a sourced fact:** calling MyEd BC "the legal IEP record". The sources say IEPs are recorded there; none we read uses the words "legal record". Some districts may keep IEPs elsewhere, since templates are not mandated. See [decision 0002](../decisions/0002-myeducation-bc-is-the-legal-record.md).
